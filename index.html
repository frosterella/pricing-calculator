The Profitable Artist Equation™

Find out what each of your pieces really earns you, what you should be charging, and whether your goal fits the hours you have. Type in the yellow boxes. Everything else calculates for you.

How it works

How to use it (about 30 minutes)

  1. Start with why: list your personal monthly costs and choose how much of them your art should cover. That's your income goal.
  2. Set your hourly rate, profit margin and the studio hours you really have each week.
  3. Add your pieces with their price, direct costs and time. Estimates are fine. The two examples show how it works.
  4. See what each piece contributes every month.
  5. List your business expenses. Use “per year” for things like insurance. They come out of what your pieces bring in, and what's left goes toward your income goal.
  6. Check your month and whether your goal fits your hours.

What the verdicts mean

  • Keeper After costs and overhead, this piece earns at least the hourly rate you want.
  • Raise the price It earns 60–99% of your rate. A price increase or a faster process will probably fix it.
  • Rethink this piece It earns less than 60% of your rate. Raise the price a lot, make it faster, or decide it's a "love it" piece, not a "money" piece.
  • Over your hours Reaching your goal this way needs more studio time than you have each week.

A few terms

Direct costs: costs that come with each piece you sell (materials, packaging, fees, firing).

Overhead: costs you pay every month even if you sell nothing. Split across every piece you sell.

What's left: price minus direct costs. This pays your overhead, then pays you.

Profit Price: the price that covers your costs, pays your hourly rate, covers your share of overhead, and adds your profit margin.

Price to fit your hours: the lowest price that reaches your income goal if you fill your studio hours with that piece.
